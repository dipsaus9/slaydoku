## Description

<!-- SECTION:DESCRIPTION:BEGIN -->
Outcome: a written, evidence-based proposal for size-banded SOLVABLE_TIERS requirements (all six tiers, both {6,7} and {9,12} bands, recalibrated relative to each other) and a normalization approach for score v2's 'squares' part -- not yet wired into any live table, that is SLAY-13.2/13.3's job.
Type: spike
Branch: SLAY-13.1/size-band-calibration-spike

Why this needs delivery-time experimentation, not a desk decision: concrete new numbers require generating and measuring a meaningful sample of puzzles per size band per tier (reusing/extending src/engine/difficulty/calibrate.ts, which is a live, working calibration engine whose CLI/dataset was dropped but whose logic is intact -- see docs/difficulty/README.md's 'rebuild it around the puzzles you keep' note -- and src/engine/generator/ladder/measure.ts, a measurement harness for the ladder generator). This mirrors the two prior calibration rounds cited in src/engine/generator/tiers.ts's comments (CAD-4.8 on 9x9, CAD-4.24 across 6x6/7x7/9x9/12x12/16x16), which planning cannot reproduce from the desk.

Scope note on both bands: do not anchor {9,12} at today's flat numbers and only tighten {6,7}. Today's flat absolute caps (e.g. medium's maxSquaresFromCards <=14) are themselves proportionally TIGHTER on a big grid, since a room/row/column clue naturally leaves more open candidates the bigger the grid is -- likely why honest very-easy/easy puzzles are already hard to produce on 12x12 today. Measure both bands against a shared difficulty scale and rebalance in both directions.

## Implementation Notes

<!-- SECTION:NOTES:BEGIN -->
Methodology: measured with a throwaway script (generateLadder + tryGenerateForScene, both live generator entry points) on generated themed scenes, one board size at a time, 25 seeds per (ladder tier, size) and 12 seeds per (hard/expert tier, size 9/12; ADVANCED_SIZES has no 6/7 today so none measured there). Ladder measurements pulled straight from LadderReport.ladder.steps (the same object SOLVABLE_TIERS' caps are checked against); hard/expert measurements used puzzleScoreV2()/computeMetrics() (the same scoreV2 the pack gate checks). Full per-size stats (n, mean, p50, p90, max) are in the raw run output; summarised below.

LADDER TIERS - measured means (squaresFromCards / chain / cardsPerPlacement / placeableAloneShare) by size, today's flat caps in parens:
- very-easy (cap sq<=4/last3, chain<=2, minAlone>=2): sq 6=1.45 7=1.49 9=1.63 12=1.72; chain 6=0.34 7=0.37 9=0.49 12=0.57; cards flat at 1 (guided generation targets exactly 1); aloneShare 6=0.58 7=0.57 9=0.52 12=0.49 (share falls with size but absolute headroom, share*people, stays 3.5-5.8, always >> minAlone 2).
- easy (cap sq<=6/last3, chain<=2): sq 6=1.40 7=1.59 9=1.83 12=1.96 (p90 3/3/3/4, but MAX hits the flat cap of 6 on every single size - the cap is binding uniformly, not just on big boards); cards 6=1.27 7=1.26 9=1.30 12=1.30 (flat, not size-driven).
- easy-medium (cap sq<=9/last3, chain<=3): sq 6=1.25 7=1.36 9=1.42 12=1.76 (max 6=4 7=8 9=6 12=8: 7x7 and 12x12 both already reach near the cap, 6x6/9x9 don't); cards 6=1.67 7=1.62 9=1.52 12=1.47 (DECREASES with size).
- medium (cap sq<=14/last3, chain<=3): sq 6=1.43 7=1.49 9=1.87 12=1.87 (max 6=4 7=7 9=13 12=13: both 9x9 and 12x12 ride right up against the 14 cap); chain 6=0.84 7=0.96 9=1.04 12=1.07 (rises with size); cards 6=1.90 7=1.80 9=1.69 12=1.64 (DECREASES with size); aloneShare 6=0.067 7=0.091 9=0.138 12=0.15 (rises with size).

KEY FINDING (drives the whole proposal): squaresFromCards is the one metric that rises consistently and monotonically with size in EVERY ladder tier. cardsPerPlacement does the opposite (falls with size, or is flat) in every tier where it isn't fixed by definition. chain rises mildly with size in easy-medium/medium only. placeableAloneShare is mixed (falls with size for very-easy/easy, rises with size for easy-medium/medium). So the size correction should concentrate on maxSquaresFromCards/lastSquaresFromCards (+a smaller move on maxChain for the two harder ladder tiers), and should NOT tighten maxCards for {6,7} - the data argues against that lever, it would fight the grain of the evidence.

AC#3 - both directions confirmed:
- {9,12} over-shoot direction: confirmed. On easy, easy-medium and medium, the {9,12} sizes' MAX observed squaresFromCards sits at or within 1-2 of today's flat cap (easy max=6=cap on both 9 and 12; medium max=13 vs cap 14 on both 9 and 12), while {6,7}'s max sits well below the same cap (easy max=6 too, but medium max is only 4/7 vs cap 14). A flat absolute cap is a much smaller share of a 12x12's ~144 open squares than of a 6x6's 36, so "leave <=N squares" is objectively a harder ask on the big board - exactly the owner's second hypothesis. Practically this shows up as candidates clustering near the ceiling on 12x12, not as outright generation failures (my sample's ok rate was 100% everywhere, generation isn't failing today, but the accepted puzzles are pushed harder against the boundary).
- {6,7} under-shoot direction: real but narrower than hypothesised, and NOT uniform across metrics. squaresFromCards and chain are genuinely lower (organically easier) on 6x6/7x7 in every tier, supporting under-shoot. But cardsPerPlacement is HIGHER on 6x6 than on 12x12 in medium (1.90 vs 1.64) and easy-medium (1.67 vs 1.47) - i.e. small-board puzzles need denser (more-cards-per-step) placements on average, the opposite of "small board is simply easier" on that one dimension. placeableAloneShare is also lower (fewer easy starting footholds) on 6x6 for medium/easy-medium. Report this honestly: the "6x6 feels too easy" complaint is real and shows up clearly in squaresFromCards/chain, but it is not a blanket "small board is easier on every axis" - cardsPerPlacement and placeableAloneShare argue the small board is locally denser/less generous in exactly the tiers where references/chains matter (medium, easy-medium). This is why the proposal below does not touch maxCards or minPlaceableAlone per band, only maxSquaresFromCards/lastSquaresFromCards (+ maxChain for the two harder ladder tiers).

HARD/EXPERT (score v2, ADVANCED_SIZES = [9,12] only - there is no {6,7} hard/expert population today, CLAUDE.md restricts hard/expert to 9x9/12x12 by product decision, so this spike could only measure the {9,12} band):
- hard: 9x9 scoreV2 mean 83.0 (p50 83, p90 85, max 86); 12x12 mean 82.25 (p50 82, p90 84.9, max 85). hardestLevel constant at 4 on both (by construction of the tier). stepsPerPerson 9x9=4.33, 12x12=4.37 (close).
- expert: 9x9 scoreV2 mean 91.8 (p50 92, p90 94, max 95); 12x12 mean 93.1 (p50 92.5, p90 95.9, max 96). hardestLevel constant at 5 on both. stepsPerPerson 9x9=3.88, 12x12=4.40 (a real ~13% gap, biggest size signal found for advanced tiers).
- Both hard and expert sit comfortably on their own side of the existing global scoreBand split (51-87 hard / 88-100 expert) on BOTH sizes, and the 9-vs-12 gap (<=1.3 points on a 0-100 scale, n=12/cell) is within likely sampling noise. Confirms the epic's claim that assessTier's hard/expert branch (tiers.ts lines ~121-129) has zero size signal: it only reads solveAdvanced's maxTechnique level (level<5 -> hard, else expert), score v2 is never consulted there at all - the score band is only enforced downstream, in the *generation* gate (qualityGate/scoreBandProblem), not in the tier a finished puzzle is assessed as. So today two different puzzles with the same technique level get the same tier label from assessTier/tierFor regardless of score or size, even though the pack/generation gate separately requires their score to fall in that tier's band. This is the mechanism gap AC1 wants closed.

very-easy: {6,7} maxCards=1 references=false maxTopShare=1 minPlaceableAlone=2 maxSquaresFromCards=4 lastSquaresFromCards=3 maxChain=2 (all unchanged - very-easy is "guided" generation that searches directly for the cap, so its distribution is a generator artifact and shows almost no organic size signal: sq p90=3/max=4 and chain max=1-2 on every single size 6/7/9/12). {9,12} maxCards=1 references=false maxTopShare=1 minPlaceableAlone=2 maxSquaresFromCards=4 lastSquaresFromCards=3 maxChain=3 (only maxChain loosened from 2->3: 12x12's measured chain max already sits exactly at the old cap of 2, giving one unit of headroom).

easy: {6,7} maxCards=2 references=false maxTopShare=1/3 minPlaceableAlone=0 maxSquaresFromCards=5 lastSquaresFromCards=3 maxChain=2 (tightened from 6->5; measured mean 1.40-1.59, p90=3, so 5 keeps a full unit of margin over p90 while excluding the puzzles that today ride the cap all the way to 6). {9,12} maxCards=2 references=false maxTopShare=1/3 minPlaceableAlone=0 maxSquaresFromCards=8 lastSquaresFromCards=4 maxChain=3 (loosened from 6/3/2; measured mean 1.83-1.96, p90 3-4, and max hits the old cap of 6 on both sizes today, evidence the true tail is being clipped there).

easy-medium: {6,7} maxCards=2 references=false maxTopShare=1 minPlaceableAlone=0 maxSquaresFromCards=6 lastSquaresFromCards=3 maxChain=2 (tightened from 9/3/3; measured mean 1.25-1.36, p90 2-3, max 4-8; maxChain tightened 3->2 since 6x6/7x7 chain p90 is 1 and max is mostly 1, a chain of 3 on a small board already feels like "medium", not "easy-medium"). {9,12} maxCards=2 references=false maxTopShare=1 minPlaceableAlone=0 maxSquaresFromCards=10 lastSquaresFromCards=4 maxChain=4 (loosened from 9/3/3; measured mean 1.42-1.76, p90 3-3.6, max reaching 6-8 already close to the old 9 cap; maxChain loosened since 12x12's chain max already sits at the old cap of 3).

medium: {6,7} maxCards=3 references=true maxTopShare=1 minPlaceableAlone=0 maxSquaresFromCards=8 lastSquaresFromCards=3 maxChain=3 (tightened from 14->8; measured mean 1.43-1.49, p90=3, max 4-7, so 8 keeps the observed max=7 comfortably inside while cutting the huge unused headroom up to 14 that let easy-feeling puzzles through as "medium"). {9,12} maxCards=3 references=true maxTopShare=1 minPlaceableAlone=0 maxSquaresFromCards=16 lastSquaresFromCards=4 maxChain=4 (loosened from 14/3/3; measured mean 1.87 on both 9 and 12, and max=13 on BOTH sizes - suspiciously identical, strong evidence real candidates are being clipped right at the old ceiling of 14; maxChain loosened since measured chain p90 already reaches 2-3, at/near the old cap of 3).

maxCards is left flat across bands for every ladder tier: cardsPerPlacement falls (or is flat) with size in this data, the opposite direction a "tighten small board" lever would need, so touching it would fight the evidence. minPlaceableAlone is left flat at 2 (very-easy only): measured absolute headroom (aloneShare * people) is 3.5-5.8 on every size, nowhere near binding at 2 on either band.

HARD/EXPERT PROPOSED MECHANISM (AC#4, new size-banded threshold since assessTier currently reads only solveAdvanced's technique level and score v2 is not consulted in classification at all):
Add a scoreV2-based split into assessTier's advanced branch (today: `result.solved && level < 5 ? 'hard' : 'expert'`), gated per size band on top of the existing level<5 pre-filter, using SOLVABLE_TIERS[].scoreBand exactly as the generation gate (scoreBandProblem) already does downstream - i.e. make classification and the generation gate agree, instead of the current two-rule split (level-based classification, score-band-based generation gate, which can silently disagree). Concretely: compute scoreV2 for the puzzle inside assessTier (or thread it in) and classify hard when score is inside the band's hard range and expert when inside its expert range, keeping level<5 as a coarse guard.
Numbers: since ADVANCED_SIZES=[9,12] means there is literally no {6,7} hard/expert population today (product policy in CLAUDE.md restricts hard/expert to 9x9/12x12), and the measured 9-vs-12 gap for both hard (82.25-83.0) and expert (91.8-93.1) is within ~1.3 points of each other and both sit cleanly inside today's global bands (51-87 hard, 88-100 expert), I recommend KEEPING the {9,12} band's numbers unchanged: hard scoreBand {51,87}, expert scoreBand {88,100}. For {6,7}, mirror the same numbers (51-87/88-100) as an unvalidated default so the mechanism has band coverage ready if hard/expert are ever enabled on 6x6/7x7, but flag explicitly: this band has ZERO measured hard/expert samples (the generator/product policy never produces them there today) and must be re-measured before it is ever turned on for real. Do not treat the {6,7} hard/expert numbers as calibrated - they are a structural placeholder only.
Whether to make scoreV2 fully replace the level<5 rule, or keep level as a pre-filter with scoreV2 as the tie-breaker within a band, is an implementation decision left to SLAY-13.2 (both are consistent with the measured data; the trade-off is level's cheapness vs scoreV2's already-duplicated-effort in the generation gate).

SCORE V2 SQUARES NORMALIZATION PROPOSAL (AC#5, src/engine/difficulty/score.ts):
Confirmed: PART_RANGES.squares = { from: 1, to: 6 } and `squares: onLadder(scaled(metrics.squaresFromCards, PART_RANGES.squares))` in scoreParts() feeds the RAW mean squaresFromCards count straight into scaled(), the only part not expressed as a share/per-person value (steps and chain already divide by people; candidates already divides by cells; cluesPerStep/references/scarcity are already shares). Tried two normalization bases against the measured means: dividing by cells (board area) collapses the metric almost entirely into a size indicator (e.g. easy: 0.039/0.033/0.023/0.014 for 6/7/9/12 - a ~2.9x spread driven almost entirely by size, with next to no separation between tiers at a fixed size) - rejected, it would make `squares` nearly useless as a difficulty signal. Dividing by `metrics.people` (equivalent to grid width, since the puzzle family is one person per row/column) is consistent with how `steps` and `chain` already normalize, and keeps a workable but much smaller size effect: easy 0.233/0.228/0.203/0.164 for 6/7/9/12 (still falls with size, but only ~1.4x spread, not ~2.9x) with cross-tier separation preserved better than the cells-based version at a fixed size.
Proposal: `squares: onLadder(scaled(metrics.squaresFromCards / Math.max(1, metrics.people), PART_RANGES.squares))`, with PART_RANGES.squares changed from raw-count {from:1,to:6} to share-of-people {from:0.12,to:0.30} (measured values across all four ladder tiers and sizes 6/7/9/12 ranged ~0.12-0.24; 0.30 gives headroom similar in spirit to today's range extending past the typical mean). This is a starting point, not a final fit - flag clearly for SLAY-13.3: after this normalization change, DEFAULT_WEIGHTS and every tier's scoreBand should be re-fitted by re-running calibrate.ts's search()/chooseCuts() over a freshly measured puzzle sample (this spike's generation harness, or an extension of it, can produce that sample), because changing one part's scale changes how it trades off against the other ten parts and where the tier cuts fall - hand-picking a new PART_RANGES value is not a substitute for re-running the calibration.
Note: this normalization does not affect hard/expert's score v2 output at all - onLadder() forces `squares` to the constant 1 for any puzzle where no ladder tier fits (ladderSolved=false), which is unconditional on the metric's value or its normalization; the AC#5 fix only changes the "squares" part for puzzles actually on the very-easy..medium ladder.

CAVEATS FOR SLAY-13.2/13.3: (1) sample sizes here are 25 seeds/cell for ladder tiers and 12 seeds/cell for hard/expert - enough to see clear, consistent directional trends but not enough for tight confidence intervals; if SLAY-13.2's own verify/tests reveal a proposed cap rejects too many or too few candidates in practice, treat these numbers as a calibrated starting point to nudge, not gospel. (2) The measurement script used here (scratch-measure.ts) was NOT committed - it is a thin, hardcoded-seed wrapper around generateLadder/tryGenerateForScene/puzzleScoreV2 with no error handling or CLI ergonomics; SLAY-13.2/13.3 should feel free to write their own harness (or ask for this one to be reconstructed) rather than treat it as reusable tooling. (3) technique-variety (distinct technique kinds fired) was not separately measured for hard/expert in this pass, only hardestLevel (constant by construction: 4 for hard, 5 for expert) and stepsPerPerson - if a finer hard/expert signal is wanted later, extending the advanced measurement to log `human.steps.map(s => s.technique)` distinct-kind counts per puzzle would be a cheap follow-up.

## Final Summary

Measured cards-per-placement, squaresFromCards, chain and placeableAlone-share for all four ladder tiers on sizes 6/7/9/12 (25 seeds/cell) with generateLadder, and score v2 for hard/expert on 9/12 (12 seeds/cell, ADVANCED_SIZES has no 6/7 population today) with tryGenerateForScene + puzzleScoreV2. Proposed concrete new per-band SOLVABLE_TIERS numbers for all six tiers (ladder caps tightened for {6,7} and loosened for {9,12} on maxSquaresFromCards/lastSquaresFromCards/maxChain; hard/expert given a new scoreV2-based size-banded classification mechanism in assessTier, with {9,12} numbers kept at today's global band since the measured 9-vs-12 gap is within noise, and {6,7} numbers flagged as an unvalidated structural placeholder since no hard/expert puzzles are generated at that size today). Proposed normalizing score v2's 'squares' part by dividing by metrics.people (consistent with how steps/chain already normalize) instead of the current raw count, with a starting PART_RANGES.squares of {0.12, 0.30} pending a full calibrate.ts refit in SLAY-13.3.
